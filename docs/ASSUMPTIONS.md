# Assumption ledger

One row per modelling assumption: what it affects, how it was tested, and what the test showed. "Not tested" is a valid entry and means the risk is open.

| ID | Assumption | Affects | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Balanced three-phase conditions; dq model with no zero sequence | Everything. Unbalanced faults cannot be studied | Not tested. Needs the abc-frame model planned for Phase 2 | Open |
| A2 | Stator and network flux transients are kept | Existence of the sub-synchronous mode | `test_series_rlc_modes_appear_at_f0_minus_and_plus_fer`: the network block gives modes at `f0 ± f_er` with decay `R wb / 2X`. Step 06 removed the transients from the linearised System B2 | Holds to 0.02 Hz and 0.2 %. Without them the sub-synchronous mode does not exist: 28 unstable cases of 54 all reported stable, growth up to 28 1/s missed. Required |
| A3 | Line is a lumped series R-L-C; no shunt capacitance, no frequency dependence of R | Resonant frequency and network damping | Not tested. A distributed-line comparison belongs in Phase 2 | Open |
| A4 | Machine circuit parameters from the classical conversion of standard parameters | Rotor resistances, and through them the negative resistance and all damping | Reactances checked at the limits `X(0)` and `X(∞)`. Time constants not compared with the exact conversion | Reactances exact. Time-constant error not quantified |
| A5 | Slip calculation averages the d and q operational reactances | Hand estimate of resonance and growth rate | Compared with the locked-shaft eigenvalue of the full electrical model over `X_C` = 0.02 to 0.62 pu | Growth rate within 2.6 %, frequency within 0.09 Hz |
| A6 | Damping-torque estimate is first-order in the coupling between electrical and torsional modes | Torsional growth-rate estimate | Compared with full eigenvalues over the same sweep | Within 0.04 1/s where growth is below 0.2 1/s. Overestimates the mode 1 peak by 86 % (7.34 against 3.95 1/s). Peak location agrees |
| A7 | No mechanical damping (`D_i = 0`) | Torsional growth rates are worst-case; small positive values appear at every compensation level | By construction. Real units have small positive modal damping | Stated, not tested. Stability margins from this model are pessimistic |
| A8 | Constant field voltage and constant mechanical torque (no AVR, no governor) | Low-frequency swing mode; negligible above 10 Hz | Not tested | Open; expected small for torsional frequencies |
| A9 | No magnetic saturation | Operating-point reactances | Not tested | Open |
| A10 | Fixed-step RK4 at 0.1 to 0.2 ms | Time-domain accuracy | Convergence study against DOP853 at tight tolerance; observed order 4.00 | Error 1.5e-8 at 0.125 ms over 0.5 s. System B2 growth rate (step 06): error 3e-6 1/s at 0.5 ms, 8e-5 at 1 ms, 0.014 at 3 ms; unstable above 4.0 ms |
| A11 | Benchmark data entered from memory of secondary sources | Every number for System A | Shaft data reproduce the five published torsional frequencies to 0.005 Hz, which pins inertias and stiffnesses jointly. Network and generator data have no such check | Shaft: confirmed indirectly. Network resistance split and generator data: to be checked against the 1977 paper |
| A12 | Induction machine data entered from memory of secondary sources (2 MW class, single cage) | Every number for System B1, most of all the boundary through `R_r` | Boundary mapped against `R_r` from 0.004 to 0.030 pu, so the sensitivity is known: 97 % compensation at 0.0055 pu, 73 % at 0.010 pu, 44 % at 0.020 pu | Sensitivity quantified. Source values to be checked |
| A13 | Single-cage rotor with constant `R_r` | Rotor resistance at slip frequency. Double-cage and deep-bar rotors have higher resistance at high slip frequency, which would lower the boundary | Not tested | Open. Direction of the error known: the model is optimistic for such rotors |
| A14 | No shunt capacitors at the plant terminals, no collector system | Operating range and reactive balance. Below about 25 % compensation the line cannot carry 0.9 pu | Observed as a pull-out limit in the steady-state solver | Sweeps start at 30 % for System B1. Step 05 added a plant-side series impedance of 0.010 + j0.15 pu to System B2: the boundary at a 10 Hz loop and rated speed moves from 21 % to 42 % compensation. Shunt capacitors still not modelled |
| A15 | Single lumped mass, constant mechanical torque | Coupling between rotor motion and the network mode | Compared with the speed held: growth rate moves by at most 0.24 1/s, boundary by at most 0.9 points of compensation | Small. A two-mass drive train is not modelled |
| A16 | The plant is one aggregated machine | Everything for Systems B1 and B2 | Step 05 varied the fraction of identical units online from 5 % to 100 % | Not monotonic: worst at 35 to 65 % online, where growth is up to 2.6 1/s above the full plant and the boundary is up to 13 points lower. Unequal units and unequal wind not tested |
| A17 | Slip calculation evaluates the machine at the real frequency `jω` | Energy accounting and `R_machine` for oscillations that are not steady | Compared with the waveform: 1.4 % for a sustained oscillation, 13 % at −2.4 1/s, 9.5 % at +6.9 1/s. At the complex frequency: at most 2.1 %. With speed held as well: 2e-9 | Holds near the boundary. Away from it, use the complex frequency |
| A18 | Ideal synchronous frame for the rotor current controller: no phase-locked loop, no measurement or switching delay | Controller behaviour near the sub-synchronous frequency; any phase lag changes how much of the gain acts as resistance | Step 04 added the loop and swept it from 1 to 60 Hz at 50 % compensation | Shifts the growth rate by −5.4 1/s at 0.75 pu speed and +1.4 1/s at 1.20 pu at the default 10 Hz. Delays are still not modelled |
| A19 | Constant rotor current references: no outer power or voltage loops; rotor speed is a parameter | Low-frequency behaviour and the dependence on operating point. With these choices the model is linear and its eigenvalues do not depend on dispatch | Step 04 freed the rotor and added outer loops | At most +0.5 1/s at the default 1 Hz outer loop. Not negligible when the outer loop is fast: +13 1/s at 20 Hz and 0.75 pu speed |
| A20 | Quick growth-rate estimate (net resistance over reactance slope) is first order in net resistance | The `σ_hand` feature | Compared with the solver against gain: 9.1 against 8.3 1/s at `Kp` = 0.01, 57.6 against 35.6 1/s at `Kp` = 0.10 | Holds for growth below about 10 1/s. The circuit root has no such limit |
| A21 | Controller gain treated as plain rotor resistance (`Ki` dropped) in the hand calculation | Hand boundary for System B2 | Largest stable gain compared with the solver over 18 compensation levels and 3 speeds | 1.9 % at the tuned `Ki / Kp` of 10.9 1/s, 19 % at ten times that, 400 % at a hundred times. Always optimistic |
| A22 | No grid-side converter, no DC link, plant connected radially with nothing else on the bus | Absolute stability limits of System B2 | Step 04 added the grid-side converter and DC link. With voltage feed-forward they move the growth rate at rated speed by 0.6 1/s | The gap to practice is not explained by them. Step 05 tested the network: plant impedance plus one parallel path as strong as the line raises the largest stable current-loop bandwidth at rated speed from 3.5 to 19.2 Hz. Limits remain relative only |
| A23 | Grid-side converter feeds the terminal voltage forward through a 2 ms filter | How much the grid-side converter damps the sub-synchronous mode | Compared with no feed-forward, and swept the filter from 0.5 to 50 ms | Largest effect after the current-loop gain. No feed-forward: −7 1/s and up to 32 points of compensation at the boundary. Slowing the filter to 50 ms: −5 to −7 1/s. Actual plant practice not confirmed |
| A24 | Converters are ideal voltage sources: no switching, no modulation limit, DC voltage does not scale the output | Coupling between the DC link and the oscillation | Not tested | Open |
| A25 | Power follows rotor speed cubed and mechanical power is constant | Operating point at each rotor speed (0.244 pu at 0.75, 1 pu at 1.20) and slow speed dynamics | Not tested against a turbine model | Open; affects low-frequency modes more than the sub-synchronous one |
| A26 | No current, voltage or rate limits | Large-signal behaviour. An unstable case grows without bound | Compensation step of 5 points: growth rate from the waveform within 0.005 1/s of the eigenvalue while the swing is below 0.6 pu | Valid for small oscillations only. Sustained oscillation amplitude cannot be predicted |
| A27 | Plant-side impedance of 0.010 + j0.15 pu and parallel paths with the line's X/R are representative values | Size of the network effects in step 05 | Swept: network resistance 0.5 to 8 times, parallel path 0.25 to 32 times the line impedance | Sensitivity known. Values not tied to a real plant |
| A28 | Network variations are studied with the current-loop model in impedance form | Parallel path and units-online results | Radial case agrees with the state-space model to 2.3e-8. Full controls agree with the current-loop model within 4 points of compensation on the radial case (step 04) | Plant impedance and parallel path since confirmed in the time-domain model: 0.0005 1/s with the added loops off, within 2.3 1/s with full controls. Units online not confirmed in the time domain |
| A29 | Dropping network transients is tested on the linearised model, not with a nonlinear positive-sequence model | The comparison in step 06 | Not tested further | Sufficient for whether the mode exists. Says nothing about large-signal positive-sequence behaviour |
